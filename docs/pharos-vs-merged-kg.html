Provenance

Merged KG — the KGB-CIA merged graph, merging code version 2.0.0, built from 22 sources (ChEMBL, ClinVar, GWAS Catalog, Disease Ontology, Human Protein Atlas, Expression Atlas and others). Final counts as merged: 1,212,415 nodes and 11,999,431 edges, which become 11,384,760 distinct non-self-loop triples after the loader deduplicates.

Pharos v319 — the Pharos 3.19 SQL dump converted to edges, then normalized through the same SRI services ORION uses: bl-lookup-sri.renci.org for predicates at biolink v4.4.2, and nodenormalization-sri.renci.org for CURIEs with conflate=true. 778,514 edges in, 696,375 out; of 330,663 distinct CURIEs, 329,150 normalized and 1,513 did not. No predicate or CURIE was mapped by hand.

Each graph was prepared against its own node table. This matters: mapping Pharos through the merged graph's node table instead keeps only 9.1% of its nodes and discards 64.7% of its edges as dangling, almost all of them compounds. Here nothing dangles in either graph.

Why the target predicate has two names

The two graphs use the same predicate name for different relations. This is the single most confusing part of the setup, so here it is in full — a census of every edge, not a sample.

Pharos's genetically_associated_with is a gene → disease edge. In the merged graph that name denotes a variant → disease edge — not one of its 771,883 subjects is a gene. The merged graph's gene → disease edge is gene_associated_with_condition. The two highlighted rows are the same relation under different labels, and those are the rows this comparison uses.

Joining on the shared name instead compares genes against variants and yields 0 shared pairs out of 74,532, because an NCBIGene: CURIE is never a DBSNP: CURIE. That would not be a worse comparison; it would be no comparison.

Neither graph was edited

Both vocabularies are left exactly as their sources produced them. The SRI predicate service resolves Pharos's source relation WIKIDATA_PROPERTY:P2293 ("genetic association") to biolink:genetically_associated_with; that is the authoritative answer and what ORION would produce. ROBOKOP independently uses the same name for variant–disease edges.

The equivalence is therefore declared at comparison time, by the --pharos-predicate flag when the split is built, and recorded in out/split_gawc319_meta.json as "pharos_predicate". It is visible and auditable in the split's metadata rather than baked into the data.

03

Method

The encoder never sees the target task. Each graph is encoded on its own edges, frozen, and only then is a decoder fitted on the shared pairs. Both graphs get identical treatment.

Step 1 — train the encoder, unsupervised

2-layer GraphSAGE with a DistMult scorer, trained by link reconstruction over all of the graph's own edges, every predicate. A positive is a real edge; a negative is that edge with its subject or object replaced by a uniformly random node. Up to 2,000 optimiser steps at 4,096 edges per step, evaluated every 200 steps against a held-back 1% slice of the graph's edges, early-stopped with patience 5.

Step 2 — freeze

The best-reconstruction state is reloaded and one forward pass emits a fixed representation per node for every node the decoder will ask about. Nothing in the encoder updates after this point.

Step 3 — fit the decoder on the overlap

A DistMult head — one learned relation vector of 128 values — over the frozen representations. Full-batch Adam, lr 0.01, up to 200 epochs, early-stopped on validation ROC with patience 20.

How the encoder and decoder work

Encoder — GraphSAGE, two layers. Each node starts as a feature vector: a multi-hot over the commonest biolink categories, plus log1p of its training degree and an is-isolated flag (50 values on the merged KG, 9 on Pharos), concatenated with a learned 64-value embedding for that node. One layer is

h' = W_self · h  +  W_neighbour · mean(h of sampled neighbours)

with LayerNorm after each layer and ReLU plus dropout between the two. Two layers means a node's representation draws on its 2-hop neighbourhood. Both graphs are far too large for full-batch message passing — the merged KG would materialise roughly 12 GB per layer — so each step samples an induced subgraph around that batch's endpoints, capped at 10 then 5 neighbours per hop while training and 25 then 10 at inference, drawn with replacement. Edges are made symmetric for aggregation, and a node with no sampled neighbour aggregates to zeros. Output is 128 values per node.

Decoder — DistMult. A triple is scored by the elementwise product of three vectors, summed:

score(s, p, o) = Σ  h_s ⊙ r_p ⊙ h_o

During encoder training r_p is one learned vector per predicate — 31 of them on the merged KG, 6 on Pharos — which is what makes the reconstruction task multi-relational rather than a plain "does an edge exist" test. In step 3 only one relation is in play, so the fitted decoder is a single 128-value r. The score is a logit, trained with binary cross-entropy.

Negatives are 1:1 in both stages. Each positive is paired with exactly one negative, so an encoder step is 4,096 positives and 4,096 negatives. They are generated differently at each stage, and the difference matters when reading section 06: the encoder corrupts either the subject or the object (a 50/50 coin flip) with a node drawn uniformly from the whole graph, while the decoder's negatives are type-matched — drawn from the observed subject and object pools — and filtered against known pairs. The encoder's task is therefore much the easier of the two.

One property worth knowing

DistMult is symmetric: score(s, p, o) = score(o, p, s), because multiplication commutes. It cannot represent edge direction. For this target that costs little — subjects are genes, objects are diseases, and no pair is scored in both directions — but it would matter for a relation whose two endpoints are the same type.

Hold-out

Because the encoder trains on edges directly, a held-out pair is removed under any predicate and in either direction — not only where it carries the target predicate. Leaving it in under another predicate would train the encoder on exactly what the decoder is later scored on, and that leak reads as a high AUC rather than as an error.

05

Evaluation protocol

  • ROC AUC on 1,558 held-out positives against 1,558 sampled negatives, per seed, 10 seeds. Chance = 0.500.
  • PR AUC reported only beside its prevalence, which is 0.500 by construction. A PR AUC near 0.5 is chance, not skill.
  • Negatives are type-matched — drawn from the observed subject and object pools — and filtered so that a negative is never a pair either graph asserts.
  • Degree baseline scored on the identical test rows, from each graph's own training edges. It is there so that "this graph wins" cannot reduce to "this graph puts more edges on hub nodes".
  • Seen / unseen breakdown of test edges, by whether both endpoints appear in the encoder's training edges.
The two runs now draw the same negatives

Negatives are drawn in the split file's own CURIE space — pools ordered by CURIE, and a filter of every pair the file lists — none of which depends on the graph being scored. Both runs therefore report the identical pools (5,052 / 6,528), the identical 762,494-pair filter, and the same negative pairs from a given seed. The comparison is paired row for row.

This replaces the earlier arrangement, where pools were built over each graph's own node ids. The same CURIE set came out in a different order, so the same RNG stream picked different entities — measured, only 0–2 of each seed's 1,558 test negatives coincided between the two runs — and a filter miss in one graph but not the other desynchronised the stream for every later row. The numbers below are from runs made after that fix; the ordering is unchanged from before it, and the magnitudes moved by less than 0.002.

The headline gap is a statement about familiar entities, which is where 93–97% of the test rows sit.

The reconstruction figures are not a quality measure and should not be read as skill. Their negatives are uniformly random node substitutions, which on graphs this size are almost never plausible edges; both runs saturate above 0.99 within 400 of their 2,000 steps. The decoder's type-matched, filtered negatives are a far harder problem, and that is where the two graphs separate.

What the gap does and does not establish

On this target, this split and this method, Pharos v319 predicts held-out gene–disease links far better than the merged KG, and the margin is not explained by degree. What produced the margin is not established by these two runs: the composition in section 01 is the leading hypothesis — 84% of Pharos's edges on the drug–gene–disease axis against 68% anatomy and expression in the merged graph, plus an 11.6× denser disease ontology — but a hypothesis is not a measurement, and no cause should be quoted from this batch alone.

The scope is also narrow by construction: gene–disease links only, on the 15,574 pairs both graphs assert, a Jaccard of 0.020. The result says nothing about the 667,010 pairs only the merged KG carries, the 79,910 only Pharos carries, or any other relation.
